A new chapter at NJIT

My starting point
is hands-on.

I’m Andrew Soliman, a first-year student studying Mechanical Engineering Technology at the New Jersey Institute of Technology.

I’m also a first-generation college student. College is a new chapter for me, and I bring a practical background into that experience: three years of trade work spanning construction, plumbing, and welding.

My interests extend from physical designs to software. I have experience with Python programming and coding with AI. My current projects include a crutch holder and a browser-based clicker game.

This portfolio follows those different parts of my background—from the work I’ve done to the ideas I’m developing now.

03 / Ideas taking shapeCurrent design project

Design with an everyday purpose

A small object.
A practical question.

Where do crutches go when someone needs to put them away?

My current design project is a crutch holder intended to help injured and elderly people put away their crutches. The model below shows the holder from four different views.

01 / The purpose

Start with the person.

The intended users are injured and elderly people who need a place to put away their crutches. That purpose gives the project a clear focus: a holder for an everyday object that its user depends on.

The project is still in progress. These views document its current model and show the design from several angles.

View the overall model ↗

02 / The shape

Look at the form.

The front view brings the purple support body and threaded bolt into focus. Viewing the same part from different directions makes its overall shape easier to understand.

A useful question for the next stage is how the holder’s shape will suit the crutch it is meant to store. That fit is something to evaluate as the design develops.

View the front of the model ↗

03 / Another perspective

Change the viewpoint.

The side view shows the support profile from another angle. Together with the front view, it offers a more complete picture of the model’s geometry.

For a practical holder, stability and ease of access are questions to consider. The model views are a starting point for examining those questions as the design develops.

View the side of the model ↗

04 / The details

Consider the connection.

This view shows the threaded opening and the separate bolt. It gives a closer look at a detail that is less visible in the assembled views.

Possible next steps include checking the fit of the parts and evaluating how easily someone can use the holder. These are directions for further development.
